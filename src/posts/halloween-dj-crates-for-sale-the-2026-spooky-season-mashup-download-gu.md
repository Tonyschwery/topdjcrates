## The BPM and Camelot Problem Nobody Talks About

Halloween sets die in the 1am hour because DJs stack horror intros and sound drops on top of tracks that don't share a key or a tempo lane. A screaming sample dropped on a track in the wrong Camelot key just sounds messy, not scary. Before your set:

- **Sort your crate by BPM range**, not just genre. Halloween sets bounce between tempos more than a normal night — plan the ramps.
- **Tag your transition edits separately** in Rekordbox or Serato so you're not digging mid-mix when the room's peaking.
- **Pre-stage your acapella outs** against instrumental beds you already know are in a compatible Camelot key, so a vocal drop doesn't clash.
- **Test your intro/outro edits at home first.** A clean cut into a horror sample sounds intentional. A sloppy one sounds like a mistake.

Doing this prep in September — before the October rush — is the difference between a smooth set and a scramble.

## What are the Amapiano subgenres in 2026?

Three lanes matter most for working DJs right now:

- **Private School** — jazzy chords, sustained pads, often vocal-led, slower and more soulful. This is your warm-up and mid-set breathing room.
- **Bacardi** — the harder, more rhythmic Pretoria style that predates Amapiano and keeps feeding back into it. More percussive, less polished, built for momentum.
- **Sgija** — the rawest and most percussive variant, dominated by hard log drums and built for peak-time energy, often the loudest thing in the room.

Knowing which lane a track lives in tells you exactly where it belongs in your night. Dropping a Sgija banger during your terrace warm-up kills the vibe. Playing Private School at 1am loses the room. **Sort by subgenre, not just by genre tag**, and your transitions start making sense again.

## How does BPM change across the subgenres?

Amapiano as a whole sits in a tight window — roughly 108 to 118 BPM, with 112 BPM as the practical center for crate filtering. But the subgenres don't sit at the same spot in that range:

- Private School tends to run toward the slower end, often closer to 108–112 BPM, giving the jazzy chords room to breathe.
- Bacardi and Sgija push toward 114–118 BPM, with tighter, punchier drum patterns that read faster even at similar tempos because the log drum sits more aggressively in the low-mid.

This matters for **Rekordbox and Serato tagging**. If your BPM field just says "Amapiano" across the board, you're guessing at transitions. Tag by subgenre and BPM together, and you can build a set arc instead of stumbling through it.

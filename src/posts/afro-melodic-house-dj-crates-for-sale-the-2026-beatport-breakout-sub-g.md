## What is Afro Melodic House?

Afro House has been called the fastest-growing genre in electronic music by the IMS Business Report, and Beatport recently promoted it to a full "Hype" genre with its own label accelerator program. Afro Melodic is the sub-current inside that boom: tracks that keep the traditional Afro House percussion — congas, shakers, djembe, tight claps — but layer in the harmonic, emotional chord work usually associated with Melodic House & Techno.

The tempo range hasn't moved. You're still working **110–122 BPM**, the same pocket Afro House has always lived in. What's changed is the top layer. Instead of a stripped-back vocal chop over a groove, Afro Melodic tracks build toward a real chord progression, often in a major key, which stands out hard in a genre historically dominated by minor keys. Producers are also playing games with production tempo versus playback tempo — tracking at 92 BPM and performing at 122 changes the swing of every hit, which is part of why these records feel slower emotionally even at full energy.

## How to spot Afro Melodic tracks in your crate

Run this checklist before you drop a track into a peak-time or sunset slot:

1. **Check the key signature.** Major key in an Afro House record is a strong signal — it's a deliberate departure from the minor-key norm and usually means melodic intent.
2. **Listen for repetition with movement.** Afro Melodic doesn't rely on constant complexity. A single filter sweep or subtle synth movement over sixteen bars is often doing more work than a busy arrangement.
3. **Watch the vocal treatment.** If the vocal carries most of the melodic weight while the synths stay minimal, that's the current formula — let the sample do the emotional lifting.
4. **Confirm the BPM pocket.** Stay inside 110–122 BPM for club programming, or drop to 118–120 BPM if you're building a sunset arc.
5. **Test the transition points.** Afro Melodic tracks often need longer blends because the harmonic tension resolves slower than a purely rhythmic Afro House cut — plan your Rekordbox or Serato cue points accordingly.

## Mixing it without killing the vibe

The most common mistake with Afro Melodic material is treating it like standard Afro House and rushing the transitions. Because the melodic content carries emotional weight, cutting too early kills the payoff the track was building toward. Give these tracks the full phrase before you bring in the next element, and use your **Camelot wheel** to stay in harmonic keys through the melodic sections specifically — a clash here reads much louder than it would over a purely percussive groove.

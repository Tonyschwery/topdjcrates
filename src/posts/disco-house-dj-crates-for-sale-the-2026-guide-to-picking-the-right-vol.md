## What makes disco house different from regular house

Disco house keeps the four-on-the-floor kick but leans on live-sounding bass, string stabs, vocal chops, and that unmistakable **filtered disco loop**. Tempo-wise it mostly sits in the 118-125 BPM pocket, which is exactly why it mixes so cleanly into funky house, tech house, and even slower Afro House cuts without a tempo slam. Camelot-wise, you'll find a lot of 8A/8B and 9A/9B material in this range — minor-key disco loops layered under major-key vocal hooks, which is why energy shifts inside disco house sets feel smoother than almost any other genre.

That smoothness is the whole appeal right now. Crowds are tired of relentless peak-time pressure. Disco house lets you hold a room at a groove instead of constantly escalating.

## Mixing tips for disco house specifically

A few things that actually matter once the needle drops:

- **Watch the vocal stems.** Disco house tracks often carry long vocal phrases. Mix on phrase boundaries, not just beat count, or you'll chop a hook mid-word.
- **EQ the low end before you blend.** Disco basslines are busy. Pulling the bass on the incoming track before the drop prevents mud, especially if you're mixing vol.2 into VOL.3 where both tracks carry heavy low-frequency content.
- **Use the filter, not just the crossfader.** A slow high-pass sweep on the outgoing track mimics the genre's own production style and makes transitions feel native instead of bolted-on.
- **Don't be afraid of long blends.** Disco house rewards 30-60 second transitions more than tech house does. The groove carries the mix.

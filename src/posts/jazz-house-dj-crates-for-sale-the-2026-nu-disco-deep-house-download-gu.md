## What actually is jazz house?

Jazz house isn't a hard genre line — it's a texture. Think St Germain's original blueprint: a 4/4 house pulse under jazz-influenced chords, live-sounding horns, syncopated basslines, and samples pulled from old jazz and funk records. In 2026 it's splitting into two lanes: a **downtempo, rooftop-set version** built for golden hour, and a punchier club version that sits comfortably at house tempo and mixes straight into deep house or nu-disco.

Either way, the DNA is the same: warm chords, groove over aggression, and space in the arrangement for a DJ to actually work with.

## How to mix jazz house into a real set

Jazz house sits comfortably in the **118–124 BPM range**, which means it bridges cleanly into deep house, organic house, disco house, and even the slower end of Afro house. That's good news — it means your **Camelot key matching** and BPM math barely need to change from what you're already doing.

A few practical rules:

1. **Open with it, don't peak with it.** Jazz house rewards patience. Use it to build the room before you go harder, not to spike energy.
2. **Ride the long intros.** These tracks are usually built with generous instrumental sections up front — perfect for **Rekordbox** or **Serato** beatmatching without fighting a vocal.
3. **Blend on the breakdown, not the drop.** Jazz house lives in its breakdowns. Mix in and out during the horn or piano sections where the groove is exposed.
4. **Layer an acapella out from a soulful house or disco track** over a jazz house instrumental section for texture — this is where a pack with proper **acapella outs** actually earns its keep.
5. **Use it as connective tissue.** Jazz house is the glue between a downtempo opener and a full house peak — don't be afraid to use one track as a full transition bridge between sets of different energy.

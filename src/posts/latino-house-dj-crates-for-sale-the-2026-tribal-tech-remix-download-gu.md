## What's the Difference Between Latin House and Latino House?

**Latin house** leans on Afro-Cuban and salsa percussion layered over a classic 124 BPM house groove — think beach club, sunset terrace, rooftop.

**Latino house** is harder and more tribal. It takes reggaeton and dembow vocals — Bad Bunny, Peso Pluma, Karol G — and drops them into a tech-leaning house or techengue frame, usually 126–130 BPM, with tribal drum breaks and a bigger low end. It's built for a peak-time floor, not a terrace.

Both belong in your crate. They just don't belong in the same 20 minutes of your set.

## How to Mix Latino House Into a Peak-Time Set

You don't need a dedicated "Latin hour" to use this stuff. Work it in as a bridge:

1. **Start from your tech house pocket.** If you're already sitting at 126–128 BPM, a Latino house edit with a tribal percussion break slots in without a BPM jump.
2. **Match Camelot keys before you match energy.** Most of these remixes are tagged 5A, 8A, or 10A — check your current track's key and pull a compatible edit rather than guessing.
3. **Use the acapella-out version for a quick tease.** A lot of these remix packs include acapella outs specifically so you can drop the hook a cappella over a tribal loop before the full beat lands.
4. **Watch your energy curve.** Tribal tech hits harder than standard Latin house — don't drop three in a row or you'll flatten the room. One every 30–40 minutes keeps it a moment, not a theme.
5. **Have an exit ready.** These tracks often run long with extended breakdowns. Know your outro point before you mix out, or you'll be stuck riding a loop.

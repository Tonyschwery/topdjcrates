## Originals vs. edits: what's the actual difference?

An original funky house track is built to breathe. It's got a full intro, a slow filter build, a groove that develops over four or five minutes. That's exactly what you want when you're filling a room from scratch.

An **edit** is a different tool entirely. Re-drummed, re-arranged, often shortened, usually built around one hook or one drop repeated with intent. Edit packs — the kind you'll find on sites like Smash The Club or in daily edit drops like remixbanger's — exist because club DJs needed tracks that hit immediately, not tracks that develop slowly. ## Tagging your crate so the split actually works

None of this matters if you can't find the right track in three seconds under a strobe light. Before your next gig:

- **Re-tag by function, not just genre.** Add "warm-up" or "peak" to the comment field in Rekordbox or Serato so you can filter mid-set.
- **Check intro length on every edit.** Anything under 16 bars should be flagged — those are your quick-mix tools, not your beatmatch-and-wait tracks.
- **Confirm BPM and key before you load, not during the transition.** Funky house drifts more than people expect — some tracks sit closer to disco house territory at 118-120 BPM, others push toward tech house at 128. Sort your crate into sub-ranges so you're not guessing.
- **Mark your acapella-out edits separately.** These are gold for building your own transitions into Afro House or Latin House crates later in the night without needing a third-party mashup.

## What BPM range should your Afro House library actually cover?

Most sources agree Afro House sits between **118 and 128 BPM**, with the sweet spot around 124. But that range isn't one flat vibe — 118 BPM feels rolling and hypnotic, while 124-128 pushes into Tech House urgency with heavier sidechain pumping. Treating the whole genre as a single tempo is why a lot of sets stall out after 45 minutes. You need slower material for the room to settle into, and faster material to actually peak with.

This is exactly why buying one volume and calling it done doesn't work. A proper Afro House crate collection needs to span that full BPM window, deliberately.

## Matching Camelot keys across volumes

Tempo is only half the job. When you're pulling from multiple volumes in the same set, **Camelot key compatibility** decides whether your transitions sound intentional or sloppy. Before you build a set list:

- Tag every track in Rekordbox or Serato with its analyzed key the moment you import it — don't rely on memory across volumes.
- Group tracks from different crates into a single "Afro House Tonight" playlist sorted by key, not by which volume they came from.
- Favor adjacent Camelot numbers (8A to 9A, for example) or the same number across the inner/outer ring when you're bridging from, say, Vol. 1 material into Vol. 7 material.
- If two tracks share a key but sit 6+ BPM apart, use a short percussion-only intro edit to bridge the gap instead of a hard cut.

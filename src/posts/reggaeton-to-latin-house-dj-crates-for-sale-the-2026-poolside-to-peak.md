Every Latin set has the same dead zone. You open with reggaeton, the room's locked in, everyone's singing the hook — and then you have to get to Latin house without losing the floor. Most DJs either jump the tempo and kill the energy, or they stall out playing reggaeton for 90 minutes because they're scared of the bridge. Neither is a plan. It's a gap in your crate.

## Why the reggaeton-to-house jump trips people up

Reggaeton mostly lives in the **90-100 BPM corridor**, with a huge concentration of current records sitting right around 96. Latin house, depending on the crate, runs **118-126 BPM**. That's not a nudge-the-pitch-fader problem — that's close to a 30 BPM gap, and if you try to force it in one move, you get a train wreck or a hard tempo cut that drops the room's momentum instantly.

The fix isn't a magic transition track. It's **programming the bridge in your crate before you ever touch the decks**.

## The BPM math that actually works

Dance tempo doubles and halves cleanly. A track at 96 BPM shares phrasing with a track at 128 BPM when you think in half-time. That's the trick serious Latin open-format DJs use to get from dembow-paced reggaeton into four-on-the-floor Latin house without the floor noticing the seam:

1. **Start reggaeton around 92-96 BPM.** Stay there as long as the room wants it.
2. **Step up to 100-104 BPM** using a dembow-leaning edit or a reggaeton record that's already pitched hot. This is your first bridge rung.
3. **Move to 108-112 BPM** with a moombahton-tempo record or a slowed Latin house intro. This is where the four-on-the-floor kick starts creeping in under the dembow pattern.
4. **Land at 118-124 BPM** — full Latin house, full peak-time energy. The floor has been climbing with you the whole time, not jumping.

That's four moves, not one leap. Each one is mixable in Rekordbox or Serato with a standard beatgrid and a little patience on the EQ.

## Reading keys so the bridge doesn't fight itself

BPM gets you the tempo ladder. **Camelot keys** get you the harmonic ladder, and skipping this step is why some reggaeton-to-house bridges still feel rough even when the tempo math is right. Before a gig:

- Tag every track in your reggaeton and Latin house crates with its Camelot key in Rekordbox or Virtual DJ.
- Favor moves to adjacent keys (8A to 9A, for example) or the same number with a letter swap (8A to 8B) during your tempo climb — it keeps the transition feeling intentional instead of jarring.
- If two tracks in your bridge ladder clash hard on key, swap in a different cut from the same crate. With 90+ tracks per pack, you've got options instead of forcing a bad mix.

## A simple pre-gig checklist

Don't build this bridge blind on the night. Set it up before doors open:

1. Pull 15-20 reggaeton tracks and sort by BPM, lowest to highest.
2. Pull your Latin-tech or moombahton-tempo tracks and slot them into the 100-112 gap.
3. Pull your Latin house peak-time tracks and sort the same way.
4. Flag 4-5 "bridge candidates" at each tempo rung, keyed and ready, so you're never stuck mid-set hunting for the next move.
5. Load intro/outro edits specifically for the bridge tracks — a clean 8-bar intro matters more here than anywhere else in your set, because you need the extra bars to blend tempo and not just volume.

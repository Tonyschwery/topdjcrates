## Why DJs are asking how to mix Afro House into Amapiano right now

Search traffic around "Afro House vs Amapiano" and "how to mix Amapiano into a house set" has spiked hard this year. The reason is simple: the two genres share African rhythmic DNA, but they don't share a bassline philosophy. Afro House gives you a rolling four-on-the-floor groove. Amapiano gives you the **log drum** — a deep, sliding percussive low end that behaves nothing like a kick-and-bass pattern. Run both at once and you get mud. Handle the handoff right and the crowd doesn't even register the shift.

If you're building **DJ crates for sale**-grade sets for rooftop brunches, late club slots, or African weddings, this bridge is no longer optional. It's the single most requested blend of the year.

## The BPM math you need to know

Amapiano sits in a wider range than most DJs assume:

- **108–113 BPM** — deep township Amapiano, closer to the genre's roots
- **113–120 BPM** — club and festival Amapiano, built for peak time
- **112–116 BPM** — the practical sweet spot most working DJs program around

Afro House typically runs **118–124 BPM**. That overlap window — roughly 118 to 120 — is your bridge zone. It's narrow, but it's real, and it's where this transition lives or dies.

## What Is Neo Rave, Actually?

Neo Rave isn't a brand-new genre — it's a recombination. Producers are grabbing the DNA of 90s and early-2000s hard trance, hard house, and eurodance, then running it through modern hard techno production: distorted kicks, pitched vocal chops, acid lines, and the kind of relentless energy you'd expect from Charlotte de Witte or I Hate Models rather than a Tiësto radio edit. Add in "hard bounce" and you get the current Tomorrowland-adjacent sound dominating sets from Novah, Nico Moreno, and Marlon Hoffstadt this year.

For working DJs, the practical takeaway is simple: **this is a tempo and texture, not just a playlist**. Most of the tracks sit between 140 and 160 BPM, which is a big jump from the 122-128 BPM range most club and wedding sets live in. That gap is exactly why so many DJs are stuck — they hear the trend, they want to play it, and they have no idea how to get from their Afro House or Tech House set into it without clearing the floor.

## How to Mix Neo Rave Into a Tech House or Open-Format Set

You don't need to build a full 160 BPM hard dance set to use this trend. Most working DJs are better served by **short, controlled excursions** — two or three tracks at the peak of the night, then back to your pocket.

1. **Pick your entry point at the tempo midpoint.** Find a harder-edged tech house or electro house track already in your crate running 128-130 BPM with a driving, distorted low end. That's your bridge.
2. **Use a half-time or double-time transition.** Most Rekordbox and Serato decks let you nudge tempo in stages rather than one jump. Walking from 128 to 140 over two tracks feels intentional; jumping straight there feels like a mistake.
3. **Lean on acapella outs and intro edits.** A clean intro/outro edit on the hard dance track gives you room to beatmatch and EQ out the kick before it fights your outgoing track.
4. **Check your Camelot keys before you commit.** Hard trance leads are melodically aggressive — a bad key clash here is more jarring than it would be in a deep house blend.
5. **Keep your exit ready.** Don't strand yourself at 150 BPM. Have a track that can walk the tempo back down before you lose the room.

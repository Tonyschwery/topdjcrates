## What is minimal house actually, and how is it different from tech house?

Minimal house strips the arrangement down to the essentials: a rolling kick, a tight hat pattern, a percussive loop, and just enough low-end to keep bodies moving. There's no big drop to wait for. The track is the groove, start to finish.

Deep tech sits right next to it — slightly warmer, a little more swing, built for long rolling sets rather than quick highlight reels. Think sleek, percussive, relentless. It's less about the one moment and more about the hour you spend in the pocket.

Compare that to tech house proper, which still wants a hook, a vocal chop, a build. **Minimal and deep tech trade the hook for the pocket.** If your current tech house folder is all peak-time weapons, you're missing the slower-burning tracks that make a terrace set actually breathe.

## How to build a minimal house / deep tech crate that actually works

You don't need an entirely new genre folder from scratch. You need to retool what you already have and fill the gaps with groove-first material. Here's the practical approach:

1. **Audit your tech house crate for groove, not just drops.** Pull out the tracks that are mostly build-and-release with little in between. Keep anything with a strong, repeatable loop section.
2. **Sort by BPM into a tighter band.** Minimal and deep tech generally sit a touch under full tech house tempo — rolling, not rushing. Group your tracks so you can hold a pocket for 20-30 minutes without a tempo jump.
3. **Key-match aggressively.** Because these tracks are loop-driven, clashing keys are more obvious than in a vocal house set. Camelot-match everything before you touch the deck.
4. **Keep intro/outro edits long.** Minimal thrives on slow, patient blends — 32 bars minimum. Short radio edits will fight you here.
5. **Save your vocal hooks for transitions, not drops.** Use acapella-style elements sparingly, as texture rather than a centerpiece.

## Common mistakes DJs make moving into minimal

- **Playing it too fast.** Minimal isn't about energy through tempo. Resist the urge to nudge the pitch fader up to "keep the energy."
- **Over-mixing.** Fewer, longer blends beat constant quick cuts. Let tracks breathe.
- **Treating it as background music.** Minimal still needs intention. The groove is the hook — program it like one.
- **Ignoring the room.** Minimal rewards patient crowds. Read the floor before committing a whole hour to it.

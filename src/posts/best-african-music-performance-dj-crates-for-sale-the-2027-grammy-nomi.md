## Why Grammy season actually matters for your booking calendar

Nomination week creates a short, predictable spike in requests. Guests who don't follow Afrobeats or Amapiano day to day suddenly do, because the names are everywhere — radio, playlists, group chats. That means:

1. **Requests skew toward the nominated artist's biggest record**, not necessarily their newest one.
2. **Guests expect a clean, danceable version** — not a 6-minute album cut with a slow intro.
3. **Open-format and wedding sets need these tracks blended into existing flow**, not played as a standalone block.
4. **The window is short.** By the time the actual ceremony airs, the moment has usually passed and attention moves on.

If your crate isn't ready now, you're catching up during the gig instead of playing it confidently.

## How to build a nomination-ready crate before the announcement drops

Here's the practical build order, the same one we'd run before any high-visibility awards week:

1. **Pull the confirmed big names first** — Tyla, Burna Boy, Wizkid, Asake, Davido, Ayra Starr, Tems. You already know these artists convert on the floor; don't wait for the trophy to prove it.
2. **Sort by BPM and Camelot key**, not just by artist. Afrobeats and Amapiano tracks often sit in overlapping ranges, so you want them grouped for quick harmonic transitions, not scattered across your library.
3. **Grab intro/outro edits and acapella outs** wherever you can. Clean entry and exit points matter more during a request-heavy set than during a planned-out night.
4. **Separate your Afro House from your straight Afrobeats.** The genres blend on record but mix very differently — Afro House needs the slower, groove-driven build; Afrobeats needs the vocal hook up front.
5. **Test the transitions beforehand.** Don't discover a key clash or a bad tempo jump live in front of a dancefloor that's watching for a specific name.

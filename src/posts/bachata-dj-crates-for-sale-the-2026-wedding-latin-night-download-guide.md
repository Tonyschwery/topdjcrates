## What BPM Should Your Bachata DJ Pack Be In?

Bachata typically sits in the 120–130 BPM range, but it's felt in a slower, syncopated groove, which is why it reads differently than house or reggaeton at a similar number. That mismatch is where a lot of DJs get burned mixing bachata live:

1. **Tag tempo and feel separately.** A 128 BPM bachata track and a 128 BPM tech house track are not interchangeable just because the number matches.
2. **Group by energy, not just BPM.** Romantic bachata for dinner and first dance. Bachata moderna for warm-up. Sensual bachata for peak floor moments right before you flip into reggaeton.
3. **Camelot-key your transitions in advance.** Bachata's melodic, guitar-driven arrangements punish a sloppy key clash far more than a four-on-the-floor house track will forgive.

If your current library has bachata dumped in with "Latin misc" and no key or BPM tags, that's the first fix before you even think about buying new packs.

The real skill isn't playing bachata in isolation — it's moving in and out of it without losing the floor. Here's a workable routine:

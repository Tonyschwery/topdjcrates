## BPM and Key Matching for Latin Genre Blends

Here's the practical issue: dembow, cumbia, reggaeton, and Latin house all sit in overlapping but distinct BPM lanes, and genre-blending albums like Tropicoqueta make it worse by switching styles mid-record. You can't rely on genre tags alone. You need the actual BPM and Camelot key printed on the file, or you're eyeballing it on the fly under club lights.

A few things to check before you build a Latin Grammy-season block:

1. **Sort your folder by BPM first, genre second.** Dembow-reggaeton hybrids often share tempo with house tracks more than with older reggaeton.
2. **Flag the vallenato and cumbia tracks separately.** Their accordion lines and live percussion don't always sit well under a four-on-the-floor kick — plan your transitions in or out accordingly.
3. **Keep acapella versions on hand.** Nominee hooks are what the crowd wants to hear, and an acapella-out lets you drop a familiar vocal over a different, better-mixing instrumental.
4. **Pull intro/outro edits for anything over 4 minutes.** Album cuts aren't built for DJ sets — you need clean mix points, not a 45-second intro before the drums hit.
5. **Pre-tag everything in Rekordbox or Serato before the gig.** Award-season requests come in fast and mid-set; you don't want to be key-matching on the fly.

## What is Afro Bashment, actually?

Afro Bashment sits at the intersection of three sounds:

- **Afrobeats** — the melodic, mid-tempo West African sound built on syncopated log drums and call-and-response hooks.
- **Bashment/Dancehall** — Jamaican riddim culture, chant vocals, and that unmistakable stutter-snare swing.
- **Afroswing/UK Rap** — trap-adjacent 808s and half-time flows that give the fusion its low-end weight.

Tempo-wise, most Afro Bashment sits in the **100–112 BPM** window, which is exactly why it slots so cleanly between afrobeats and dancehall in an open-format set. You don't need a genre-jump edit to get there — you need records that were built to bridge both worlds from the jump.

## How to mix Afro Bashment without killing your energy

The mixing itself isn't complicated if your files are properly prepped. Here's the practical checklist:

1. **Sort by BPM first, key second.** Afro Bashment's sweet spot (100-112 BPM) overlaps with slower amapiano and mid-tempo dancehall — use Camelot keys to confirm harmonic compatibility before you commit to a blend.
2. **Use the acapella out.** Most Afro Bashment vocals are chant-heavy and repetitive — a clean **acapella out** lets you lay the hook over a dancehall riddim without fighting two full instrumentals.
3. **Watch the drum pattern, not just the tempo.** Log drums and dancehall stutter-snares can clash if you EQ carelessly. Pull the low end on the outgoing track before you bring in the new kick.
4. **Keep intro/outro edits handy.** A track with a proper 16 or 32-bar intro saves you from chopping on the fly in Rekordbox or Serato mid-set.
5. **Test the transition at soundcheck, not on the night.** Afro Bashment's swing feel doesn't always read the same on a club system as it does on headphones — trust your ears over the waveform.

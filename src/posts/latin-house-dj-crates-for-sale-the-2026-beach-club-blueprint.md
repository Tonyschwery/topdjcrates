## What BPM and key range to expect

Latin house mostly sits in a tight, DJ-friendly pocket:

- **122-126 BPM** for house-leaning cuts and Bad Bunny-style crossovers
- **124-128 BPM** for the Latin-tech hybrids with rolling basslines and Latin percussion loops
- **95-100 BPM** for the occasional half-time reggaeton-to-house edit, useful as an intro or breakdown moment

Camelot-wise, expect a lot of tracks clustered around **8A/8B and 9A/9B** — minor-key, moody, sunset-friendly territory. Run your key column next to BPM in Rekordbox or Serato and you'll spot the compatible runs fast. **Harmonic mixing** matters more here than in straight tech house, because Latin house leans on chord stabs and vocal hooks that clash hard if you're a key apart.

1. **Open with a low-energy Latin house cut** — something around 122 BPM with a light vocal or instrumental hook. Let the room settle into the groove before you introduce a lyric-heavy track.

## Common mixing mistakes to avoid

- **Don't rush the tempo.** Latin house crowds want groove, not a tempo climb. Stay in the 122-128 range for longer than you think you should.
- **Don't ignore the vocal.** A lot of Latin house edits carry a strong Spanish vocal hook. Clashing two vocal tracks on top of each other is the fastest way to muddy a transition — use **intro/outro edits** that isolate the vocal drop.
- **Don't skip the reggaeton bridge.** If your crowd is mixed, dropping a straight reggaeton track between Latin house cuts (rather than forcing everything into 4/4) keeps energy honest.

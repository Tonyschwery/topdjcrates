## What counts as "deep house" in 2026?

Ask ten DJs and you'll get ten answers, and that's fine — deep house has always been a mood more than a strict genre code. Right now the trending sub-flavors are:

- **Afro Deep** — deep house structure with African percussion and log-drum-adjacent low end
- **Deep Tech** — deep house warmth crossed with tech house groove and drive
- **Broken Beat / Balearic Deep** — looser swing, dubby chords, made for sunset-to-dusk transitions
- **Classic Vocal Deep House** — soulful top-lines over stripped-back low end, the "singalong at 2pm on a rooftop" sound

That overlap matters for your crate strategy. You don't need a genre-pure "deep house only" folder. You need tracks that **bridge** deep house into the genres you already play, so you can drop into it and climb back out without a train wreck.

## How to build a deep house set without killing your momentum

The mistake most DJs make with deep house is treating it like a separate set instead of a **gear change**. Here's the practical way to fold it in:

1. **Anchor around 120-124 BPM.** That's the pocket where deep house, Afro house, and organic house all overlap in Camelot-compatible keys, so you can move between them without a jarring tempo jump.
2. **Use it as a bridge, not a wall.** Drop deep house between your Afro house block and your tech house block — it softens the transition instead of forcing a hard cut.
3. **Lean on vocal tracks for the room, instrumentals for the mix.** Vocal deep house reads well on a dancefloor; instrumental and dub versions give you clean space to layer acapellas from your other crates.
4. **Watch your energy curve.** Deep house is built for a slow build. Resist the urge to peak too early — save your biggest room-movers for after the deep house block, not during it.
5. **Check your outros.** Deep house tracks often have long, DJ-friendly intro/outro edits by design — use them. They're built for exactly this kind of long, patient blend.

Halloween gets all the mashup-pack attention every October. Meanwhile, **Día de los Muertos** has quietly become one of the biggest bar and club bookings of the season — and most DJs are still crating it like it's just another spooky-season night. It isn't. It's a completely different crowd, a completely different tempo range, and a completely different crate.

If you've got a gig on November 1st or 2nd, or a "Día de los Muertos weekend" booking squeezed between Halloween parties, this is the breakdown you need.

## What makes a Día de los Muertos set different from Halloween?

Halloween sets are built on horror mashups, acapella-out edits, and novelty drops. Día de los Muertos is a celebration of life, family, and ancestry rooted in Mexican culture — and the music reflects that. Think mariachi and banda reinterpretations blended into modern production, corridos, cumbia, and a heavy dose of reggaeton and Latin house for when the dancefloor wants to move rather than mourn.

Billboard and Beatsource both run dedicated Día de los Muertos playlists every year, and the crossover is real: tracks that started as traditional folk songs now show up remixed at 100-128 BPM, sitting comfortably next to current reggaeton and Latin house releases. That's your opening. **Blend tradition with tempo**, and you'll hold a floor that a generic Halloween crate can't touch.

## Building the Día de los Muertos DJ crate

Your crate needs three tempo lanes, not one:

1. **Warm-up (85-98 BPM)** — corridos, banda-leaning tracks, and cumbia edits to set the tone while the room fills and the altar photos go up.
2. **Mid-set groove (95-115 BPM)** — reggaeton and dembow-tempo Latin tracks that keep energy climbing without forcing a tempo jump.
3. **Peak-time (118-128 BPM)** — Latin house and Latino house cuts that turn a themed night into an actual party.

## How to actually program the night

Don't just load a folder and hit shuffle. Work the room in stages:

- **First 45 minutes:** low-tempo corridos and cumbia-leaning tracks. Let people take photos at the altar, greet friends, settle in.
- **Next hour:** step into reggaeton. Keep transitions on the one, use your acapella outs if you've got vocal-driven corridos you want to blend into a reggaeton instrumental — it's a crowd-pleasing move that respects the tradition without stalling the energy.
- **Peak hour:** go Latin house. This is where [Top Latin-House](https://topdjcrates.com/music?crate=11) earns its keep — the percussion-forward production sits naturally under reggaeton vocals if you want to layer rather than hard-cut.
- **Last 30-45 minutes:** bring it back down slightly, or close on a crowd-favorite reggaeton anthem. Know your room — some Día de los Muertos bookings want a hard peak-to-close, others want a gentler wind-down that respects the holiday's reflective side.
